🎙️ About Voxal

What it does

Voxal is an instant push-to-talk voice room. Create a room, share its code or link, and anyone who joins can talk to the others, like a walkie-talkie. Camera, screen sharing and chat are there when you want them. No account is needed.

How it works

  • Peer to peer. Voices travel directly between participants over WebRTC. Audio never goes through a media server.
  • The room code is the host. The host helps everyone find each other, and if the host leaves, another participant takes over so the room stays alive.
  • Push to talk. Hold the mic button (or your shortcut on desktop) to talk, or double-tap for hands-free.
  • Firewalls. When a direct path is blocked, an encrypted relay forwards the traffic without being able to hear it.

Where to find it

  • web.voxal.app: in any browser
  • Desktop app: macOS, Windows and Linux, with a global push-to-talk shortcut
  • iOS and Android apps
  • Source code: open source on GitHub

Voxal app and voxal.app

This push-to-talk app and the voxal.app portal are two parts of one product. The app works fully on its own. The portal is optional: connect your account with Connect to see your organization's channels and who is in them, join a channel in one tap, and use your organization's relay for firewalled networks. The app does the talking; the portal knows who is around.

Blur what is behind you, or replace it. Applies to your camera only, and you are picked out of the picture on this device — no frame is ever sent to a server to do it.

The chosen scene on its own, drawn on this device. Drag it, or swipe on a phone, to look around the room (in a call you can do the same on your own picture). The full-screen button at its bottom right shows it over the whole screen, at its own shape, with the scenes and the light at the top right.

How an animated scene is lit. Match my room lights it like the light your camera sees on you, so a dim room gets an evening with the lamps on. A time of day, the clock or your screen’s light or dark mode keeps it that way whatever your room. People who draw your scene themselves see the same light.

With an animated scene, sends just you and a thin edge of your room; the people you call draw the same scene on their own device. Uses less upload in calls that go peer to peer. They may see the scene slightly differently, and it costs their device some work. Has no effect with blur or your own image, or when video goes through the relay.

Captures your camera at 1080p, draws the background at that size and sends it that way: sharper for you and for everyone you call, at the price of more work on this computer and about four times the upload per person. Restarts your camera when switched. A computer that cannot keep up is stepped down on its own.

With an animated scene, the room behind you turns as you tilt or turn your phone, the way a real one would. Uses the phone's motion sensor; nothing is recorded or sent anywhere except the angle itself, to people who draw your scene.

How far the blur reaches, as a share of the picture — so it looks the same whatever your camera's resolution. Applies while the background is set to Blur, and takes effect immediately, even mid-call.

How hard the cut-out between you and your background is. Softer hides the odd stray pixel and suits hair, glasses and a cluttered room; sharper keeps a crisp outline and suits a plain wall. Applies to the blur and to background images alike, immediately, even mid-call.

How often you are picked out of the picture again. High follows fast movement more closely and costs more battery; Battery does the opposite. A device that cannot keep up is stepped down on its own whatever you choose here.

In a dim or backlit room, brightens the copy of the picture the detector looks at — never the picture anyone else sees — so it can still find your outline. Does nothing at all in ordinary light.

For a camera that does not move. During a call, open the background menu on your own video and choose Capture empty room: a countdown gives you time to step out of the picture, and a click tells you when you can come back. The cut-out is then checked against that picture, which sharpens the edge around hair and shoulders — as long as the camera stays where it was. The picture stays in memory on this device, is never sent or saved, and is forgotten when you switch this off. It is set aside on its own if the camera moves or the light changes.

⚙️ Advanced

Advanced troubleshooting & self-hosted deployments.

TURN relay credentials

A TURN server relays your audio when a firewall blocks a direct peer-to-peer connection. Get a free pair from metered.ca → TURN Server (an app-name subdomain and an API key).

Fallback relay

Used only when no credentials are set above, so peers behind strict firewalls can still connect.

Video routing

Camera and screen-share video can route directly between participants (peer-to-peer) or, for large rooms, through Cloudflare's relay servers, which forward — and can technically see — the video. This never applies to voice, which always stays peer-to-peer.

Network usage

How much data Voxal is moving right now, and what it is carrying. Only meaningful during a call.

Jitter buffer

How much incoming audio to buffer before playing it. Higher values smooth out choppy audio on a lossy or relayed connection, at the cost of a little delay. Automatic raises it on its own when a link starts dropping packets.

80 ms

0 ms leaves it to the browser. While Dev mode is on and you are the room host, this value is also pushed to everyone in the room (peers who set their own value keep theirs).

Shown in the room once your browser has asked for the microphone on two separate visits — it cannot remember the grant for you, so the tip names the setting that makes it stick.
