Camera: one encode per peer

Under the p2p-only preference this harness pins, camera is a full mesh too — so a sharer encodes and uploads one stream per other peer, at roughly twenty times an Opus stream. The shipped default may route this through an SFU above two participants instead, and that topology is not measured here. This machine ran out of room. One browser was encoding every peer's camera at once, and when that happens the frame rate falls first — while the encoder still blames its own bitrate cap. Read the rows below as a ceiling of the harness, not of the app.

Timings

Joining a call already in progress
Signaling opens in 36 ms, and the first audio arrives at 246 ms. The clock starts at the join, not at browser launch — a real user already has a browser open.
Losing the host
A new host is elected at 7099 ms and audio is back at 7917 ms — the same moment, give or take, and both are simply the ~7 s heartbeat timeout. The room is leaderless for seven seconds; it is never silent, because audio connections to non-host peers are never torn down during a migration.
Joining a room that is already on camera
The room is audible at 322 ms and every camera in it is on screen at 586 ms. Hearing and seeing are separate numbers: a picture needs its own call per sharer, a keyframe and a decoder, and the mesh builds them one at a time.
